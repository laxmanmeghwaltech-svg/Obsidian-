
# 🏗️ The Structure of a Good LinkedIn Post
> [!ABSTRACT] Overview
> Strong LinkedIn posts follow a consistent shape. Once you understand the six-section framework, you can write high-performing content on demand.
> 
## 1. The Hook 🪝
The first one or two lines of your post.
 * **Purpose**: It is the only part seen in the feed before the "see more" button; it must stop the scroll.
 * **The "Big Three" Qualities**:
   * **Surprise**: Give the reader something they didn't expect.
   * **Pain**: Name a problem the reader is currently feeling.
   * **Reward**: Promise a specific benefit for reading on.
 * **Warning**: If your hook is vague or sounds like a hundred other posts, it isn't a hook.
## 2. Pain Points and Frustrations ⚡
Once you get the click, you must make the reader feel seen.
 * **Method**: Name specific problems, frustrations, or roadblocks.
 * **Specificity**: Don't be abstract. Instead of "Marketing is hard," use "You spend three hours writing a post that gets twelve likes".
 * **Result**: The more accurately you describe their world, the more they trust you.
## 3. Actionable Value 💡
This is the teaching phase where you provide something the reader can use immediately.
 * **The 24-Hour Rule**: Give value specific enough to be applied within the next day.
 * **Avoid Generic Advice**: Instead of "Be authentic," give a tactic like "Open every post with a sentence shorter than ten words".
 * **Authority**: Best content comes from your own experience—what worked and what didn't.
## 4. The Dream Picture ✨
Paint a concrete picture of what life looks like after applying your tactic.
 * **Function**: Shifts the reader from thinking about the *work* to the *reward*.
 * **Visuals**: Show the version of themselves on the other side—e.g., more inbound leads, a full calendar, or a business that runs without them.
## 5. The Engagement Question ❓
Ask one specific question to invite a real answer.
 * **The Goal**: Get a comment without making the reader think for too long.
 * **Good Questions**: Pointed and personal (e.g., "What's the one piece of advice you think is actually wrong?").
 * **Bad Questions**: Too broad (e.g., "What do you think?").
## 6. The CTA (Call to Action) 📣
Close with one clear instruction.
 * **Rules**: Never stack multiple CTAs. Pick one specific action: follow, save, or repost.
 * **Example**: "Follow me for more posts on building a personal brand without burning out".
 * **Avoid**: "Like, comment, share, follow, and let me know what you think".
