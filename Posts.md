109 versions of one infographic.

I'm not proud of that number.
But I'm also not embarrassed by it anymore.

Version 1 looked fine. Version 12 looked better.
Version 109 is the one I actually posted.

And the next one took 30 seconds.

That's the part nobody talks about when they
say "I made this with AI in minutes."

They skip the weeks of building the thing
that makes the minutes possible.

Most people treat AI design like a vending machine.
Put in a prompt, get out a graphic.

That's why their content looks different
every single week. The vending machine
doesn't remember your brand.

There are 3 places you can be right now:

Prompting flat images and hoping.
Generating HTML layouts and burning tokens.
Running a system that already knows your brand.

Most people are in the first group.
A lot think they're in the second.
Almost nobody is in the third.

Here's what being in the third actually took:

Brand rules written down as a SKILL.md file.
Not vibes. Actual hex codes, Tailwind classes,
spacing grids. If it wasn't documented,
the AI defaulted to generic.

HTML templates with CSS variables already set.
So every output starts from your brand,
not from scratch.

A local design repo with the real assets.
Logos, headshots, brand imagery.
All sitting there, ready to pull in.

QA sub-agents that review every output
before I even look at it:

• Is the typography hierarchy right?
• Are the color ratios on brand?
• Is the spacing consistent?
• Is the visual density too heavy?

Below 85, it regenerates on its own.
Above 95, it comes to me.

I didn't write any of that in code.
All of it was built through conversation.

109 versions taught the system what good looks like.
480K impressions ran on what it learned.

The infrastructure took days.
Each graphic now takes less time
than reading this post.

That's the actual investment thesis.
Not the tool. The system behind the tool.

The full playbook breaking this down
is in the comments.

Where are you sitting right now,
Level 1, 2, or 3?

And what's the real reason
you haven't built the system yet?
